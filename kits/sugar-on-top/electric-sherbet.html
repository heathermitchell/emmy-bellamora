The Scene Behind It

A rooftop.
Golden hour.
Music you feel.

A scene, not a painting — built from feeling first.

Someone just handed you a drink that’s the color of a sunset — orange bleeding into hot pink. The music is loud enough to feel in your chest. You’re wearing something that makes people look twice. You don’t care. Actually, you do care. You love it.

Electric Sherbet is for the person who’s tired of being told to keep it professional. Professional is beige. She’s not beige. She’s been careful for long enough and the brand that doesn’t look like her is the brand she can’t get behind.

Tangerine Fizz is not construction-cone orange — it’s party orange. The orange of a fresh cocktail catching sunset light. Fuchsia Shock is not bubblegum — it’s the neon sign in the window that says “yes, tonight, here.” Lime Volt is the ingredient that takes “warm brights” and makes it Electric Sherbet.

Twilight Grape holds it all. The sky behind the rooftop. Gives the brights somewhere to land. Without the depth, there’s no contrast. Without the contrast, there’s no pop.

The Whole Box.
Every color material.

Eight colors. Each one named for what it actually is. Not “orange.” Not “pink.” Tangerine Fizz. Fuchsia Shock. Lime Volt. These colors aren’t decorating — they’re saying something.

Primary Bright
Tangerine Fizz
#FF6B35

Fresh-squeezed orange with sparkling water. Electric, warm, alive. Not construction-cone orange. Party orange — the color of a cocktail catching golden-hour light.

Signature Pink
Fuchsia Shock
#E8356D

Hot pink that didn’t ask permission. The neon sign in the window. Confident without trying. This color self-selects — people who love it know immediately.

Citrus Yellow
Lemon Zap
#FFD23F

Sunshine concentrated. The fizz at the top of the drink. Effervescent and sparkling, tiny light points dancing. Pure optimism in a color.

Deep Warm
Raspberry Velvet
#8B1A4A

Where the party gets intimate. Fuchsia’s older sister — she’s been here before and she’s staying late. Plush matte velvet, deep and rich.

Electric Accent
Lime Volt
#B8E62E

The surprise ingredient. The thing that makes this palette pop instead of just “bright.” Citrus meets neon, almost luminescent. Without it, warm brights. With it, Electric Sherbet.

Cool Contrast
Twilight Grape
#4A2060

The sky behind the rooftop. Deep matte with subtle shimmer, giving all this energy somewhere to land. Without the depth, the brights have no pop.

Warm Neutral
Sherbet Cream
#FFF0E0

The palate cleanser. Warm cream with the faintest peach blush — smooth and matte. Breathing room. The surface that makes the brights readable.

Dark Ground
Midnight Mango
#2A1525

The darkest dark — warm, fruity even in shadow. Not black (black would kill this palette). Dark satin with warm undertone visible in light. Holds the whole thing together.

Three fonts.
The Rooftop Suite.

Space Grotesk brings the energy. Bricolage adds the warmth. DM Sans does the work. All sans-serif — no serifs would fight this palette. All free Google Fonts, forever yours.

Headline · Space Grotesk 700
LOUD.
Space Grotesk 700, 72pt · Midnight Mango
Subhead · Bricolage Grotesque 400

The sentence that makes someone lean forward. Bricolage shifts personality at different sizes — playful small, powerful big. The warmth that keeps geometric from going cold.

Bricolage Grotesque 400, 28pt · Fuchsia Shock on Sherbet Cream
Body · DM Sans 300–500

DM Sans earns the name. Soft geometric sans that reads clean without feeling corporate. The rounded openness matches Electric Sherbet’s energy — friendly, approachable, not intimidating. At light weights on dark it’s effortless. The rounded terminals add warmth that pure geometrics wouldn’t bring.

DM Sans 300, 16pt · Midnight Mango on Sherbet Cream
The rule:
Space Grotesk brings the energy. Bricolage adds the warmth. DM Sans does the work. Let them each do their thing.

Color in context,
never in isolation.

A color swatch on white tells you nothing. What matters is how a color behaves next to other colors. These are the signature pairings — the combinations Electric Sherbet was built for.
