The Textures

Everything Is Material.
Even the Flat.

These aren't just hex codes — each color carries a printing history. Lithograph. Letterpress. Screen-print. Aged paper. The textures are about what comes off a press, not what grows in a garden.

Cabaret Magenta

Flat matte lithograph print. Solid, opaque, no gradient. The color that doesn't hedge.

Charcoal Ink

Letterpress. Slightly embossed, visible ink density variations. The bold line that doesn't hesitate.

Cream Playbill

Old poster paper. Slightly yellowed, the ghost texture of printed-over-printed. Warm with history.

Zinc Matte

Smooth concrete or zinc printing plate. Industrial, flat, neutral. The street. The wall everything gets pasted on.

Absinthe

Screen-print neon. Slightly transparent where the ink is thin, vibrating. The color that crackles.

Ochre Stage

Aged paper with foxing spots. Warm and worn. The stage floor. The spotlight warmth. Grounding without being boring.

Midnight Velvet

Deep matte with the faintest grain. Like a dark stage curtain. The darkness before the lights come up.

Blush Cancan

Fabric texture. Like a ruffled petticoat — directional, light-catching. The personality without the punch.

The Lautrec Note

The Most Impactful
Poster Is Restrained.

"The most impactful poster is restrained. Fewer colors. One good silhouette. Everything at full volume." — The principle behind this kit

Lautrec invented modern branding. Logos, brand identity, visual advertising — he helped build all of it. The technique is deceptively simple: strong outlines, flat color, limited palette. Maximum impact from minimum elements.
