The Textures

Nothing Is Flat.
Everything Is Material.

Van Gogh pressed the paint into the canvas with his whole hand sometimes. These colors carry that — each one a material, not just a hex. The impasto ridge. The wet paint. The canvas showing through.

  • Amber Glow

    Thick impasto, visible brushstroke ridges catching light. Honey held up to a window. The center of every swirl — applied with pressure, left with intention.

  • Plum Vortex

    Deep matte velvet with directional grain — like brushstrokes all pulled in one direction. The gravity that holds the swirl together. Rich, magnetic, unapologetic.

  • Coral Flame

    Slightly glossy — wet paint, fresh, just-applied energy. The underside of a cloud on fire. The most alive color in the box. Used in small quantities because it insists.

  • Brushstroke Gold

    Textured metallic-paint — not smooth, not metallic. Gold paint layered thick with a palette knife. Van Gogh's stars, warmed by sunset. Optimistic without being naive.

  • Rose Dust

    Soft matte, like pastel chalk on textured paper. The sky between sunset and twilight — where coral cools down and plum warms up. Gentle but not weak.

  • Burnt Umber Swirl

    Raw linen with oil paint soaked in — earthy, organic, warm. The underpainting. Where depth lives without coldness. The warm-value extreme; ideal for text-heavy layouts.

  • Canvas Cream

    Actual canvas texture — woven, visible thread, warm. The ground the paint lives on. Clean space that still feels like part of the painting.

  • Twilight Slate

    Smooth matte stone, cool to the touch, architectural. The village silhouette beneath Van Gogh's sky. Structural, quiet, necessary. The one cool anchor in an otherwise warm painting.
