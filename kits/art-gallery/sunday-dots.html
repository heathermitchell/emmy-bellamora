The Textures

Nothing here is flat.

Pointillism is a material discipline — Seurat pressed paint into canvas with precision, dot by dot. Every color in this kit carries the same logic: a surface quality, a texture, a material truth.

Coral Confetti
Stippled dot on dot — the pointillist technique itself. Small, uniform, energetic. Each dot placed.
Parasol Navy
Woven fabric. Like a tightly stitched parasol canvas — visible weave at close range.
Linen White
Actual linen. Visible crosshatch weave, warm. The canvas before the first dot is placed.
Afternoon Haze
Smooth matte with atmospheric depth. Not paper, not fog — light before it lands on anything solid.
River Sage
Soft matte with organic irregularity. Hand-pressed paper with botanical inclusions.
Marigold Dot
Matte with tiny raised dots. Like braille or embossed paper. Every dot earns its place.
Peach Pixel
Smooth matte. Like matte photo paper — warm and even. The quiet version of coral.
Plum Point
Dense, deep matte. Ink that's dried on heavy stock. The shadow that proves the light.
Promenade Stone
Fine sandstone, slightly gritty, natural. The park path that holds everything else in place.

Sunday Dots is the kit for the detail person. The one who notices when something is two pixels off. Who has opinions about grid spacing. Who believes that presence — being actually present for every decision — is how you make something beautiful.

Precision with personality. Not rigid. Not sparse. Pointillism is about density — thousands of dots, each its own color, creating richness. This kit should feel full, layered, generous with detail.

Seurat's original is soft greens and blues. Sunday Dots takes the same dot-by-dot precision and fills it with confident, modern color. The coral stays coral. The navy holds everything together. The third direction — River Sage — does the cultural work. The big picture was always there in the noise.
