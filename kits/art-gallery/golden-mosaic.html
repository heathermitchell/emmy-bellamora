The Textures

Nothing Is Flat.
Everything Is Material.

In Klimt's work, gold leaf is pressed over oil paint. The surface has depth, irregularity, history. These colors are built the same way — each one a material, not just a hex.

  • Hammered Gold

    Not shiny-smooth gold. Hammered, textured, ancient — gold that's been worn and handled and still catches light. The unifying thread of the whole collection.

  • Byzantine Teal

    Glazed ceramic tile with hairline crackle — the weight of a mosaic tile from a Mediterranean palace, worn by a thousand years of salt air.

  • Ivory Tessera

    Old marble with the faintest warm veining. The blank tile that rests the eye between all that richness. Warm ivory, faintly golden — nothing stark here.

  • Lead Came

    Oxidized lead — matte, granular, slightly irregular. The structural metal that holds the stained glass tiles in place. Not the grout, not the tile: the metal behind both.

  • Rose Quartz

    Crystal texture — slightly translucent, soft-focus, warm glow from within. The skin tone in Klimt's figure work. The reminder that a person lives inside all this pattern.

  • Mosaic Midnight

    Smooth dark stone — polished granite, dense basalt. The grout between the tiles. Without this, the mosaic has no structure. This color is the edge that defines everything around it.

  • Mulberry Wine

    Crushed velvet with directional pile — deep and light-absorbing. The jewel in the center of the brooch. The color that makes you look twice.

  • Antique Bronze

    Oxidized metal with verdigris hints — the patina of old jewelry, the wood of the box that holds it. Warm without being bright. Gold's earthier, quieter cousin.

  • Pale Gilt

    Gold leaf over textured surface — translucent, catching light unevenly. Gold diluted with sunlight. The lightest metallic: shimmering but soft, precious but not heavy.

The Inspiration

Every Square Inch
Is a Different Pattern.

In The Kiss, Klimt painted rectangles on his robe and circles on hers. Gold leaf layered over oil paint. Every surface covered with something different. It shouldn't work. It works because the gold is the unifying thread — no matter how many patterns he layers, the gold runs through all of them like a single note that everything harmonizes with.


This kit is built the same way. The gold makes it one thing. The patterns make it everything else. Maximalism with taste means: not everything thrown at the wall — everything placed with intention, so densely that the intention itself becomes the aesthetic.

The gold was always the answer.
